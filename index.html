North Carolina Lets a 17–3 Lead Slip Away in 28–20 Loss at Clemson

The Tar Heels outrushed the Tigers 214–186 and held the ball for more than 33 minutes, but a 135-yard passing day and two long Clemson runs in the fourth quarter decided it.

CLEMSON, S.C. — North Carolina led Clemson 17–3 and 20–15 on Saturday and still lost 28–20. The Tar Heels outrushed the Tigers, held the ball for 33:32 and forced two turnovers, but they were outscored 22–3 in the second half at Memorial Stadium, and Clemson finished on a 25–3 run over the final 38:36.

Carolina had the game where it wanted it for most of the first 30 minutes. The defence held Clemson to 117 yards in the first half against freshman quarterback Tait Reynolds, who was making his first career start, and the offence ran for 214 yards at 5.5 a carry over the afternoon. What it lacked was a passing game. Billy Edwards Jr. went 13 of 30 for 135 yards, and Carolina managed only 154 total yards after halftime to Clemson’s 284.

Two long runs decided it. Gideon Davidson went 45 yards up the middle on the first play of the drive after Carolina’s late field goal, and Tyler Brown’s 34-yard touchdown on third down made it 28–20 with one minute remaining. Those two plays accounted for 79 of Clemson’s 186 rushing yards.

A first half that went to plan

Demon June opened the scoring with a 3-yard touchdown run, and Edwards found Benjamin Hall from 15 yards out to make it 14–3. Carolina had a chance to add more when Greg Smith forced a fumble and Jaylen Harvey recovered it at the Clemson 25, but Aeron Burrell missed a 47-yard field goal. He converted from 33 yards later in the second quarter, and UNC went into the break up 17–6 with 195 yards of offence to Clemson’s 117.

Takeaways that went nowhere

The second half turned on a sequence that began well. Smith intercepted Reynolds at the UNC 2-yard line, a play that should have flipped field position. On the next snap June fumbled, and Vic Burley recovered for Clemson at the UNC 12. The defence held the Tigers to a field goal, so the damage was limited to three points, but Carolina had gone from an interception at its own 2 to handing Clemson a short field in a single play. Between that and the missed 47-yarder, neither of UNC’s takeaways produced a point.

June’s fumble did not lose the game on its own. It was the clearest of several moments when Carolina could have taken control and didn’t.

A run game that worked, a passing game that didn’t

Hall carried 18 times for 130 yards, a 7.2 average, and also caught the 15-yard touchdown. June added 57 yards and a score on 12 carries, and Edwards and Jaylen McGill combined for 27 more. Clemson never found a consistent answer, and Carolina finished at 5.5 yards per carry.

Edwards’ 43 percent completion rate was the offence’s biggest problem. Jordan Shipp caught six passes for 68 yards, half of Carolina’s passing total, while Jelani Thurman was targeted nine times and finished with three catches for 18 yards. Once it was clear the Tar Heels couldn’t punish them through the air, Clemson could load the box against Hall and June and send pressure when Edwards had to throw. UNC went 5 of 14 on third down, and the passing struggles made its third-and-long situations especially hard to convert.

Freshman receiver CJ Sadler was ruled out on the final availability report. He caught three passes for 66 yards against East Tennessee State, entered Saturday with four catches for 73 on the season, and was one of Carolina’s more significant young vertical threats. It’s impossible to say how much he would have changed the result, but without him Clemson had less reason to respect anything deep.

Two runs and a weather delay

Carolina led 17–15 and had second-and-goal at the Clemson 7 with 8:11 remaining when lightning stopped play. When the game resumed, June was stopped on two straight runs and Burrell kicked a 22-yard field goal to make it 20–15. A touchdown there would have forced Clemson to score twice. Instead, the margin stayed at one possession.

Clemson answered immediately. Davidson’s 45-yard run put the Tigers deep in UNC territory, and two plays later he scored from 8 yards out for a 21–20 lead, Clemson’s first of the day. Carolina then failed to convert a fourth down on its next possession, and Brown’s 34-yard touchdown run on third down closed it out.

A defence that held until it didn’t

Smith finished with an interception and a forced fumble, Xavier Lewis had two sacks, Harvey added another, and Derek McDonald made 10 tackles. Reynolds started 5 of 9 for 17 yards and went 15 of 20 for 198 the rest of the way, finishing 20 of 29 for 215 yards and a touchdown. Carolina allowed 5.2 yards per carry overall, which is manageable. Giving up 45 and 34 on two plays is not, particularly when the offence is scoring as rarely as UNC’s was.

The Tar Heels were also without senior defensive end Melkart Abou-Jaoude, who hurt his hand against TCU, was questionable all week and was ruled out on the final availability report. He has been one of the line’s more disruptive players. Carolina still pressured Reynolds without him, but the absence cost the defence depth and versatility. It can’t be pinned directly on Davidson’s and Brown’s runs.

The quarterback alternatives

Edwards’ day will raise the question of who else could play, but neither option is an easy sell. Miles O’Neill, a redshirt sophomore, has seen only limited action this season, and true freshman Travis Burgess has yet to establish himself in a college game. Handing either an ACC game with the outcome still in doubt is a real gamble, and unless the staff believes O’Neill can run the offence at a higher level, Edwards remains the more logical choice.

What has to change in the bye week

Passing efficiency comes first. The offence doesn’t need to become pass-heavy, but Edwards needs easier completions early in drives, and Carolina needs ways to get Shipp and Thurman into space so defences can’t commit to Hall and June. Finishing drives is second. UNC scored on all four of its red-zone trips but reached the end zone on only two, and it can’t keep settling for field goals when a two-score lead is available. On defence, the priority is preventing the explosive run.

There is no need for a rebuild. The run game works, the defence controlled long stretches, and the team played physically against Clemson. What Saturday exposed was how thin the margin is when the passing game can’t produce. Carolina returns to Chapel Hill after the bye to host Notre Dame on Oct. 3.
